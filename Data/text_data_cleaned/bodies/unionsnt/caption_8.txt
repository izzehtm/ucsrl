Workers will now face enormous pressure as they attempt to implement new Child Protection laws within an already overstretched workforce and without any additional funding. 

The sector has consistently warned that meaningful reform requires investment in prevention, early intervention, workforce capacity and culturally appropriate services—not simply changes to legislation.

Since these laws were introduced, workers, unions, Aboriginal community-controlled organisations, legal experts and community service providers have raised genuine concerns about the impact they will have on vulnerable children, families and the frontline workforce.

Those concerns have been reinforced by the draft recommendations of the Country Liberal Party-commissioned Webb-Shanahan Review, which demonstrate that the issues affecting child safety lie within the department's operations, not the legislation itself.

Rather than listening to those concerns, the CLP Government rammed through dangerous laws and went even further by labelling workers in the sector as "morally bankrupt."

Unions NT Secretary Erina Early said the comments were offensive, reckless and an insult to every worker who has dedicated their career to protecting Territory children.
