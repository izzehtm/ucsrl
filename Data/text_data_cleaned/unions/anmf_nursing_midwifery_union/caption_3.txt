New data showing the number of healthcare workers hospitalised after being assaulted at work has more than doubled in a decade confirms years of warnings about worsening safety of healthcare workers, say the Australian College of Nursing (ACN) and the Australian Nursing and Midwifery Federation (ANMF).

National figures released by the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare show assault has now climbed from the fourth to the second leading cause of hospitalisation for injuries among healthcare workers. By comparison, assault ranks seventh for the general Australian population.

“This should not be a wake-up call, because nurses and their colleagues have been sounding the alarm about this for years,” said ACN Chief Executive Officer, Adjunct Professor Kathryn Zeitz FACN. “But this data definitively shows that there can be no more excuses for inaction.”

“Nurses, midwives and personal care workers continue to be attacked, abused and intimidated in their workplaces. We also know that workplace violence and aggression (WVA) is underreported, so the true scale of the problem could be much worse,” ANMF Federal Secretary Annie Butler explained.

“Urgent action, including improved staffing levels, and additional security measures, is needed, so nurses, midwives and personal care workers are safe on the job. There must be zero tolerance for any form of WVA against frontline health workers,” said Ms Butler.
