A United Workers Union survey of almost 1,000 early childhood educators found 90% say a lack of inclusion support compromises the safety of all children in their care, while 87% say they are regularly unable to give children the education and care they deserve.
 
For ACT educator Leane Townrow, the problem became impossible to ignore. After working in a for-profit childcare centre, she moved to a not-for-profit service because she became increasingly concerned about what she was seeing.
 
"In my experience, it was never about the educators, the families or the children in the space. It was always about the numbers on the page matching up with their profit needs."
 
When children with additional needs don't receive the support they require, educators are left trying to fill a gap they didn't create.
 
"When the staffing isn't there, educators are left carrying a gap they didn't create, and it's the children, families and educators who rely on these services who bear the cost."—UWU Early Education Director Carolyn Smith.
 
Every child deserves the support they need, and every educator deserves the staffing to provide it.
